Ground and window: the rules

Thesis. The picture decides the band. A photograph of the place, wide and sharp enough, stops being a rectangle in a column and becomes the band's GROUND, with the heading set into its darkened edge (the home hero's recipe). A photograph of a person is shown through the building's own window: the 1929 tower's pointed lancet, head and shoulders, whole. A scan from the archive is hung as a museum PLATE. Everything else sits BESIDE its text at the text's height. Nothing sits in a column below its words.

Reference moves. HP Pres's arch mask (but our arch is the lancet, radius 1.5 spans, where theirs is round-shouldered); Washington National Cathedral showing its building at three distances, which here maps to the three treatments: the ground is the building seen whole, the window is a person seen through it, the plate is the building as it was.

Classifier (build time; inputs: asset WxH from the ref, alt, eyebrow, cleaned body, page position)

a       = W / H                                   // from image-<id>-<W>x<H>-jpg
old     = /\b(1[5-8]\d\d|19[0-4]\d)\b/ in splitStega(alt).cleaned or eyebrow   // a year before 1950
legend  = a >= 1.8 && body has a para matching /left to right/i
          immediately followed by a list of 3..8 items

if a <= 0.85                       -> portraitsSeenOnPage++ == 0 ? WINDOW : FRAME
elif legend                        -> LEGEND
elif a < 1.25 && old               -> PLATE
elif a >= 1.3 && W >= 2000 && groundOK(position) -> GROUND
else                               -> ROW

groundOK(i) = groundsOnPage == 0
           || (groundsOnPage == 1 && i - lastGround >= 4 && bandsOnPage >= 6)
           // at most two per page, the first eligible wins, the second only far away
           // and never on consecutive bands; never the page's first band after a photo hero

LEDE (every branch) = first block is a normal paragraph
           && 8 <= words(cleaned) <= 40          // the length guard: a 70-word paragraph stays body
           && ends in . ! ? : (optionally a closing quote)   // "Nursery Care (104)" is a label, not a lede
           && something is left in the body after it
BODY flow   = Ledger's: label paras (<= 5 words, no end punctuation) + following paras -> LABELLED rows;
              lists -> ruled rows; h3 -> sub-head rule; 2+ paras <= 180 words at full width -> SET (2 cols);
              beside a picture -> one MEASURE column (a two-column set in six columns is too narrow)
side        = imageSide (left | right); the WINDOW defaults to left so the text keeps column 6,
              the column a headingless continuation band picks up

What renders per branch, and why

  • GROUND: the photo is 100cqw wide (the container-query width of #main, never 100vw), 52cqw tall (520 to 780px), cover at 50% 25% (higher than the site's 40% head-room rule, because the bottom third is under the gradient and the words anyway, so the crop spends its loss there). The hero's two gradients, same stops, same ink. Tick, eyebrow, h2 and lede sit bottom-left at the container's left edge, so the heading keeps the page's one left edge. Caption (alt) is the first thing on paper, then the rest of the body in Ledger's flow. Why: a wide view has room in its sky or floor for words; putting the words there is the only way a landscape stops being "a huge rectangle".
  • WINDOW: the head above as usual, then the lancet (4 columns, max 340px wide, 1 : 2.1) beside the lede and body. The photo starts 16% of the way down the window and fades over its top 7% into the band's own surface colour (the --band custom property, inherited from the band, so paper or soft in either theme with no build-time colour), leaving the point of the arch open under the gold moulding, so the point of the arch never cuts a crown: the crown sits where the arch has already widened to over half the span, the face where it is over 80%. A gold moulding line follows the arch 9px outside it. Why: portraits were the worst images on the site because a landscape frame takes the head; the lancet is taller than any portrait, so it can only ever lose the sides.
  • FRAME (second portrait on the same page): the same beside-row, the picture at its native portrait shape (max 560px tall), square-topped, with the same gold moulding. Never a second arch: two arches on a page is the HP Pres identity, not a moment.
  • PLATE: the scan at its native shape inside a mat of the OTHER surface (soft on a paper band, paper on a soft band), a gold line 6px off the print, the caption inside the mat as the museum label. Never bled, never cropped: an archival print's edge is part of the document.
  • LEGEND: described above. The list paragraph and the footnote stay where the editor wrote them, under the photo; the rest of the body follows as a SET.
  • ROW: the fallback. Body in columns 1 to 6, the photo in 8 to 12 at its native shape (no forced 16:9), tops aligned with the lede, sticky while the text runs longer than it. No moulding: a view of the place is not hung, it is looked at.

The family resemblance (rule 17), held in all six branches

  • The tick. One 28px gold rule. Every caption hangs from it; on a ground it sits above the eyebrow; in the legend each name hangs from one. It is the only ornament the photo system adds.
  • The caption. Always the alt text, italic Castoro 15px, muted, directly under the picture's bottom-left corner. Never over the photo (except nothing: the ground's caption is on paper under it).
  • The gold line. Pictures of people and of the past are hung: one 1px gold line set off them (window, frame, plate). Pictures of the place are not: the ground is the wall itself, the row is a view.
  • The edges. Headings always at column 1, above. Pictures sit on column 1, 6, 8 or the viewport edge. Square corners everywhere; the one lancet per page is the only shape.

Seven photo bands in a row (History's seven eras)

History is mostly scans and portraits, so a typical run reads: PLATE right, WINDOW left (the first pastor with a portrait), PLATE left, FRAME right (the next portrait), PLATE right, GROUND (the 1929 building, if a sharp wide photo of it exists), ROW or PLATE. What keeps it from monotony: (1) imageSide alternates, and the classifier never overrides it except for the window; (2) the three shapes of object (mat, arch, frame) differ in silhouette even on the same side; (3) the one ground, if any, lands in the middle, because the first ground-eligible band wins and eras 1 to 3 are scans. Honest limit: if all seven are scans it is seven plates alternating sides, which is a museum wall; calm, but a wall.

Porting cost

  • New pure module src/lib/photo-shape.ts (about 120 lines): aspect from the asset ref, the year test, the legend test, the lede guard, and a page-level pass that assigns WINDOW/FRAME and the ground budget. photo-shape.test.ts covering the seven bands and the budget cases.
  • SectionRenderer.astro (foundation): must run the page-level pass once (ground budget, first portrait) and hand each ImageText its shape. About 15 lines. It already walks the sections for the spare-image pool, so the pass rides that loop.
  • ImageText.astro rewritten as five small branches (about 260 lines) plus Lancet.astro (the clipPath, the moulding SVG) and about 120 lines of CSS in globals.css. spare-images.ts untouched, but a borrowed spare changes the picture and so may change the branch: the classifier must run on the picture actually rendered.
  • Preview risk: alt and eyebrow go through splitStega().cleaned before the year test; word counts likewise. The legend test reads list items and a paragraph's text: cleaned only. The ground's text on a photo is still click-to-edit (plain elements). Moderate risk: the page-level pass means editing one band's image in the preview can flip ANOTHER band's branch (a new portrait earlier on the page takes the arch). That is correct but surprising; say so in the Studio help text.
  • Parity: every ImageText render changes; recapture after review. Rule 19: the ground uses 100cqw, the reflow gate with real scrollbars covers it.

Honest weaknesses

  • "Clear subject" is not derivable. The ground test is aspect and resolution only. The home interior photo passes and works; a 2400px photo of a crowded fellowship hall would also pass and put a heading over faces. Mitigation: the text sits bottom-left under a 94% gradient, which darkens that corner whatever is there. A real fix needs a field, which the brief forbids.
  • Portrait framing assumes a head near the top. True of every headshot and studio portrait on the site; a full-length portrait with a tiny head would show the head small in the arch, not cropped. The window never crops the top, only the sides.
  • The year test is crude. "Pastor since 1921" on a modern photo of a plaque would read as archival if square. Cost of a false positive: a matted modern photo, which still looks fine.
  • The legend assumes people evenly spread. Five equal columns under five people is close here; with a clumped group the names drift from their faces. It is still in reading order, which is what "left to right" promises.
  • The window is a strong move. If the church dislikes it, it is the one piece that fails loudly. It is also the piece that gets the reaction.
